Fifth alpha, and a large one. Nyctr now collects from your sources on a schedule without being asked, saved sources can be edited and removed, the screens say what they do not know, and temporary access is ended on time and kept on record from before anything is sent to a source.

Still a prerelease. Read "Upgrading" before you install it: after this upgrade Nyctr starts collecting by itself. Nothing in this release was run against a real source by this build; see "Not verified".

## What changed

### Sources

- **Each source shows its own logo** on the Sources page, the connect form, Blocks, Inventory, the Briefing and the Map's device panel. Colour still names the source on lines and on the Map.
- **Collection runs on a schedule, set in the console.** On Setup, under "This installation", a switch and a list of intervals from 5 minutes to 24 hours. It is on by default at every 15 minutes. Source cards and the Briefing say when each source is next collected, or "Only when you press Collect" when the schedule is off. A change applies without a restart and is recorded in Activity.
- **A scheduled collection that fails is tried again** after about 1, 2 and 4 minutes before the source is shown as failed, for failures that may pass: a dropped connection, no answer in time, a request limit, an error at the source. A refused key or a rejected setting is shown at once. A retry never runs at or after the next scheduled collection, so on a 5 minute schedule only two fit. A Collect you press yourself is not retried.
- **A saved source can be edited.** Its name and numeric settings (such as how many hours of lookups to read) can change at any time. Its address, site or profile, and secret name can change only until its first successful collection; after that, add a new source to read from somewhere else.
- **A saved source can be removed.** A preview says with counts what goes and what stays, and you type the source's name to confirm. Everything collected from it is deleted, with the device assignments and label links that hang on it. Requests, ended exceptions, ended passes and the whole Activity history are kept. Removal is refused while Nyctr still has something of its own at that source (an open exception, a live guest pass, a guest it cut off). Removal sends nothing to the source.
- **What a collection could not read is recorded and shown.** A read the key may not make, a network whose range could not be read, a gateway that could not be identified: every collection records these, the source card shows "Connected, with gaps" with what is missing and what that means, and the Briefing's "Needs you" counts it.
- **Requests through the gateway's cloud address are paced.** Nyctr sends at most 96 requests to one console in any 60 seconds, so a large site finishes a little later where it used to fail on the vendor's limit of 100. The console's own address is not paced.
- **A timeout is told as a timeout.** A source that did not answer in time, or whose connection dropped, used to be reported over HTTPS as a certificate problem, and for that reason was never retried. It is now told as what it was and retried like any other failure that may pass.
- **Refusals name the field** by its label on the form and say what to change, where they used to show a storage key or a server variable.

### Inventory, Map and Findings

- **Devices sit on their networks.** Nyctr reads each network's address range and each piece of equipment's uplink, and places a device on a network when its address is inside exactly one range. That placement is worked out by Nyctr and labelled inferred. A device no source places sits under "Network not reported".
- **The gateway is found** when the source does not list it as one, from the source's own statements: a device whose address is a network's gateway address, or the one piece of equipment outside every range that others name as their uplink. Where Nyctr worked it out, the Map panel and Inventory say "worked out by Nyctr". When the evidence is not enough, nothing is filed as a gateway and the gap is shown.
- **Counts say what they count.** The Briefing lists devices, network equipment, networks, rules, blocked addresses and sources each by name, with no mixed total. Blocks and the Briefing say how far back the stored lookups really go. Inventory and the Map count what those screens show. Activity names the source on every row about one.
- **Everything on the Map opens.** A "more" box is now a button that opens a list of every item it holds, and each row opens the same panel as a drawn card. Arriving from Inventory with an item that is not drawn opens its list and selects it. Two cards with one name from different sources each say which source they are from.
- **The Map draws only what changes** when you pick an item or open a list, so a site of several hundred items stays quick.
- **Screens say what they do not know.** The Briefing says how many sources are not collected yet or could not be collected. Findings lists the four checks Nyctr makes, marks a check that had nothing to look at, and lists what is collected but not checked, such as firewall rules. Guests says why it shows no guests (no source can tell them apart, the Wi-Fi names could not be read, or no Wi-Fi name uses a sign-in page) and does not say nobody is on. If the Briefing cannot read its list it says so and offers "Try again".

### Blocks and the member portal

- **Blocks shows one page at a time.** The latest 200 lookups, a line saying how many are stored, and "Show older" for the next 200. Filters and search ask the server, and the counts on the filter buttons are about everything stored.
- **Blocks says when lookups name no device.** One note per source whose lookups do not say which device made them, with the source's own reason, and separate empty states for no DNS source, not collected yet, could not be collected, and really none.
- **A member is told what cannot be tied to a device.** When some stored lookups name no device, the portal says it has not seen anything blocked on the member's devices and that one of theirs may be missing, with who to ask. It says "have not been collected yet" or "could not be collected" where that is the case, and tells a member with no devices that first.
- **A member's list is their latest 200 lookups**, with a line saying how many older ones are not listed. An older one is found by typing its address.
- **A member is told what really happened to a request.** After an approval that failed: "This was not opened. Whoever runs Nyctr has been told." An opening an administrator ended early says so. In Demo mode an opening says it is a demonstration and that nothing was opened. If "My requests" cannot be read it says that, and no longer says nothing was asked for.

### Temporary access and guests

- **Expired access is ended on time.** Expired exceptions are ended every 30 seconds whatever a collection is doing. Before, an exception could outlive its time by the length of a collection.
- **Opening and ending one address cannot cross.** Both take the same claim on the source and address before they call the source, an exception is ended at its source once whichever path asks, and two openings of one address share one entry that is removed with the last of them. Every change at a source ends within 60 seconds and is recorded as failed at that limit.
- **An opening is on record before it is sent.** If the source accepted the add and the opening then failed, or the source applied it late, Nyctr keeps reading the source on every 30 second pass for five minutes and removes the entry if it appears. Until then Open now lists it as "Not opened / Not confirmed" and says it may be open at the source and until when Nyctr is still checking. An address that was already allowed at the source before Nyctr asked is never removed.
- **An approval cannot stop Nyctr.** Before, saying no to a request while its approval was waiting on a source could leave Nyctr answering nothing at all. Now a second decision on a request that is being approved is answered at once in plain words, and a failed approval leaves the request waiting with a note.
- **The daily limit on automatic openings holds** for requests sent at the same moment.
- **If a source is removed while a change is being sent there**, Nyctr takes its entry back out at the place the call used. If that fails, the exception stays listed as open with what to remove by hand, and "I removed it by hand" ends it once you have.
- **A change is shown as done only where the record says it was applied.** With changes to sources switched off, which is the default, an approval used to be listed as open with a countdown; it now reads "Not changed. Nyctr is read-only for this source." A guest reads "Cut off", "Locked out" or "Online" only when that was applied at the source; otherwise "Simulated: cut off" or "Decided to cut off, not changed at the source". Activity headlines follow the outcome, a decided request says what happened at the source, and "Recently closed" says how each exception ended there.
- **The Map no longer draws a guest who is waiting on the sign-in page as cut off.**

### Wording, the demo and the Docker test stack

- **"Source" and "collect"** replace "provider" and "sync" in about 40 sentences, and every row in Activity reads as a sentence.
- **The demo uses reserved names and addresses.** Fabricated office names, lookups ending in `.example`, and address and hardware address blocks reserved for documentation. The three pictures in the README are retaken from it.
- **The Docker test stack shows much more.** A fabricated site of 321 items, sources that misbehave on request, guests, and, when started for it, changes that its fake sources accept and report back. Its certificate names changed, so an existing test volume must be reset once.

## Upgrading

- **Nyctr now collects by itself.** After the upgrade a live installation collects from every saved source on a schedule: every 15 minutes unless you say otherwise, with the first collection about half a minute after it starts. Before, it collected only when someone pressed Collect unless a deployment value was set. Change the interval or switch the schedule off in the console: Setup, "This installation". Demo mode never collects.
- **The deployment value changed its meaning.** `NYCTR_COLLECT_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (the chart's `collection.intervalSeconds`) is now only a starting interval, used until a schedule is saved in the console. A value of 0, which is still the default, used to mean "no schedule" and now means "not set": the 15 minute default applies. It does not switch the schedule off; only the console does. The chart no longer sends the variable when the value is 0. No other server setting was added or changed.
- **Collection only reads.** Changes at sources stay off unless an operator names the integration in `NYCTR_PROVIDER_WRITES`, as before, and the assistant stays off without a model. But the guest cut-off rule and lock-out act at every collection. If you have allowed changes at your network source and switched that rule on, it now acts on the schedule with nobody at the console.
- **No database schema change.** Upgrading from v0.1.0-alpha.4 runs no migration. The schedule, the time of the last scheduled run, pending retries and the claims are rows in the existing settings table.
- **`unconfirmed` is a new value in an exception's existing status.** It marks an opening Nyctr sent and has not confirmed. If you go back to the v0.1.0-alpha.4 image, that image does not list such a row as open or as closed, and never checks the source for it or removes anything; look at the source yourself. The older image also ignores a schedule saved in the console and goes back to the deployment value, where 0 means no schedule. Do not run the two images side by side on one database; the chart replaces the old copy before it starts the new one.
- **If you call the API yourself:** `GET /api/blocks` now answers an object with `rows`, counts and a `next` marker for the page after, where it answered a list. `GET /api/rules` no longer carries `collect_interval_seconds`; the schedule is read at `GET /api/collection` and saved by an administrator at `PUT /api/collection`.
- **A collection may take longer.** The overall limit on one collection went from 90 to 210 seconds, so that a paced collection through the cloud address can finish.
- The image is `v0.1.0-alpha.5` and the chart is `0.1.0-alpha.5`, the same pairing as before.

## Validated

With fabricated data only, on one Linux x86-64 machine:

- The API, release and acceptance-helper test suites, the lint and format checks, the console tests and the console build.
- A walk through the merged build in a real browser (headless Chromium) at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, before this release:
  - on the Docker acceptance stack, the production image in live mode against a fabricated gateway and DNS service, started read only and again with changes allowed: the schedule and its retries, editing and removing a source, gaps, the Map and Inventory with a 321 item site, Blocks one page at a time, approving, refusing and ending access, expiry on time across a restart, guests and passes, and a source that answers slowly or late;
  - in Demo mode, every console page and the member's pages, signed in as the administrator and as the member.
- That walk found one defect that left an address allowed at a source: when a source applied an approval after Nyctr had stopped waiting for it, the address stayed allowed there with no open exception. It found eight places that misled: the Briefing saying nothing needed attention when its list could not be read, an approval that changed nothing listed as open, simulated guest changes and a simulated opening shown as real, a waiting guest drawn as cut off, a chosen guest or request opening out of view on a phone, Guests advising to turn on a sign-in page when the Wi-Fi names could not be read, and a timeout told as a certificate problem. All nine were fixed and checked again on the merged build. In the re-check the slow source's late entry was removed in every run and the fabricated source's allowed list ended empty.
- Earlier in this cycle, with fabricated data on PostgreSQL 17: opening and ending the same address in both orders, several processes opening and ending shared addresses, a process killed after an add was sent, and a decision arriving while an approval waited on a source.

## Not verified

- **Nothing in this release was run against a real source by this build.** Tests and the walk above use fabricated answers.
- **The gateway's cloud address** was read once against one real console by an earlier build (one installation, read only). Pacing, the cloud's error answers and sharing between sites are tested with fabricated answers only.
- **Changes at a real DNS source or gateway have never been run:** opening and closing an exception, guest passes, letting a guest on and cutting one off.
- **Whether the gateway is recognised on a real network.** The position rule needs other equipment to name the gateway as its uplink, and that is known only once it runs there. If it does not, the gateway stays filed as a switch and the gap shows.
- **Request cost through the cloud address on a real site,** and whether a large one stays under the vendor's limit.
- **The schedule running for days.** Tests use a clock that is passed in. The "two copies, one run" test uses two instances in one process.
- **The member's side on the acceptance stack,** which has no member account. The sentence a member reads when an administrator ends a real opening early was read in the code and its tests, not on a screen.
- The chart tests need Helm and were not run on that machine, and neither was an installation of the chart in a cluster. CI runs both for this release, with Helm 3 and Helm 4.
- Everything listed under "Not verified" in earlier releases still applies.

## Known issues

- **A guest pass cannot be issued from the console when more than one network source is saved.** The form names no source, so Nyctr takes the first by name. By API it can, by naming the source.
- **An opening at a DNS source applies to everyone on that DNS profile** until it ends. The console says so before approval.
- **For five minutes after an opening that could not be confirmed,** an entry for that address added by hand at the source is taken for Nyctr's own and removed. Do not add the address there while Open now lists it as not confirmed. A source that applies an add more than five minutes late leaves an entry that nothing on record removes.
- **Blocked lookups cannot be tied to a device when the DNS source names none,** for example when a gateway forwards every lookup to the DNS service under its own name. A member is then shown nothing to ask for, only that one of theirs may be missing and who to ask.
- **Counting risk bands on Blocks slows down** past about 60,000 stored lookups (about 1.5 seconds at 100,000), because the band is not stored and every lookup is scored to count it.
- **Open now does not refresh by itself.** A "Not confirmed" row that Nyctr has since settled stays on screen until the page is reloaded. If both the add and Nyctr's own removal are answered late, the entry is removed but the record says nothing was removed.
- **While a retry is pending,** the source card shows the time of the next scheduled collection and not the next try. Activity shows the next try.
- **Once a source has collected, where it reads cannot be changed.** Add a new source, then remove the old one. Removing a source deletes the device assignments and label links made on its devices.
- **Only IPv4 ranges are read.** A device with only an IPv6 address is not placed, and one network whose range could not be read leaves that whole site's devices under "Network not reported" until a later collection reads it.
- **Two site shapes can be filed wrongly as a gateway,** both at a site whose router is another vendor's: a core switch with an address outside every range and equipment below it, and an old out-of-range switch that one access point still names as its uplink. Either is labelled as worked out by Nyctr.
- **A connection test through the cloud address** waits at most 20 seconds for its turn. For a site that needs more than 96 requests it cannot finish there, though collections of the same site do. Two copies of Nyctr on one database pace separately.
- **A guest change that a source applies late is not followed up** the way an opening is: a guest let on late shows as on after the next collection, and a pass created late shows as the source's, not Nyctr's.
- The limits listed under "Limitations to resolve before a production release" in `docs/security-model.md`.
